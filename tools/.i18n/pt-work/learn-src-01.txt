1	Personal pronouns
2	A pronoun replaces a noun. Hun is for a woman or girl, han for a man or boy, den/det for things (den for en/ei words, det for et words). Dere is “you” (several people), de is “they”.

14	Question words
15	The question word comes first and the verb right after it: “Hvor bor du?”. Hva — what, hvem — who, hvor — where, når — when, hvorfor — why, hvordan — how, hvor mange — how many.

26	Verbs: present tense
27	In the present tense add -r to the infinitive, and the form is the same for every person: jeg snakker, du snakker, de snakker. Irregular: være → er, gjøre → gjør, si → sier, vite → vet, ha → har.
28	speak → speak(s)
29	eat → eat(s)
30	live → live(s)
31	be → am/is/are
32	have → have/has
33	do → do/does
34	I speak a little Norwegian.
35	We live in Norway.
36	Modal verbs
37	After kan, vil, skal, må and bør the verb is in the infinitive without “å” and without -r: “Jeg kan snakke norsk”. After other verbs (liker, begynner, prøver) use “å”: “Jeg liker å lese”.

46	I like to read.
47	Nouns: en, ei, et
48	Every noun has a gender: en (masculine), ei (feminine, can be replaced by en) and et (neuter). The definite form puts the article at the end: en bil → bilen, ei bok → boka (boken), et hus → huset.

50	a book → the book
51	a house → the house
52	a cat → the cat
53	an apple → the apple
54	I have a cat. The cat is called Pus.

56	Object pronouns
57	When a pronoun is the object of the action it changes form: jeg → meg, du → deg, han → ham (han), hun → henne, vi → oss, dere → dere, de → dem. “Jeg ser henne”, “Hun hjelper oss”.
58	I → me
59	you → you
60	he → him
61	she → her
62	we → us
63	you → you (plural)
64	they → them
65	Can you help me?
66	We will call her tonight.
67	Grandma is visiting us on Sunday.
68	Possessive pronouns
69	A possessive agrees with the thing owned: min bil (en), mi bok (ei), mitt hus (et), mine bøker (plural). The same for din/di/ditt/dine and vår/vårt/våre. Hans, hennes and deres never change. It usually comes after the noun: “bilen min”.

79	My/your: min, mi, mitt, mine · din, di, ditt, dine
80	The form follows the owned item, not its owner: en bil → bilen min/din, ei bok → boka mi/di, et hus → huset mitt/ditt, plural → vennene mine/dine.

88	Our/his/her/their: vår, vårt, våre · hans, hennes, deres
89	Vår agrees with the item: vår bil, vårt hus, våre venner. Hans, hennes and deres do not change. Deres can mean plural “your” or “their”; context decides.

101	Bilen min or min bil? Word order
102	Both orders are correct. After the noun: definite noun + possessive — bilen min, boka di, huset vårt. Before the noun: possessive + indefinite noun — min bil, di bok, vårt hus. The post-noun pattern is very common; front position often stresses the owner.

104	MY car
105	OUR house
106	It is MY car, not his.

108	Reason, result, addition and contrast
109	Linking expressions show the logic of a text. Derfor, likevel and dessuten are adverbs: when placed first, the finite verb follows directly (Derfor sparer Liv). Ettersom introduces a subordinate clause with subject before verb. På grunn av and i motsetning til take a noun or pronoun. For å + infinitive explains purpose.
110	therefore — result
111	nevertheless — surprising contrast
112	moreover — additional information
113	since/as — reason in a subordinate clause
114	because of + noun
115	in order to + infinitive — purpose
116	unlike / in contrast to + noun
117	The rent is high. Therefore Liv makes a budget.
118	The jacket is cheap. Nevertheless, Mia does not buy it.
119	The kitchen is modern. Moreover, the living room has large windows.
120	Liv waits since she wants to think first.
121	Leo overslept because of a flat phone.

124	Verbs: past tense
125	The past tense (preteritum) is for finished actions in the past: “I går spiste jeg pizza”. Regular verbs take -et, -te, -de or -dde: snakket, spiste, levde, bodde. Many common verbs are irregular: gå → gikk, se → så, være → var, ha → hadde, gjøre → gjorde, si → sa, komme → kom.

128	live → lived
129	go → went
130	be → was
131	have → had
132	see → saw
133	come → came

136	Adjectives
137	An adjective agrees with its noun: en stor bil, et stort hus (+t), store biler (+e in the plural). In the definite form it always takes -e: den store bilen, det store huset. Some never change: bra, gratis, moderne.

145	Prepositions
146	I — inside (i Norge, i Oslo, i huset), på — on and at many places (på skolen, på jobb, på butikken, på kino), til — to (til byen), fra — from (fra Ukraina), med — with/by (med bussen), om — about (om morgenen — in the morning).

155	Word order (V2)
156	In a Norwegian statement the verb is always in SECOND place. If the sentence starts with something other than the subject (i dag, nå, i morgen), the subject moves after the verb: “Jeg går på jobb” → “I dag går jeg på jobb”.

158	Now I am having breakfast.
159	Tomorrow we are leaving.
160	In winter it is cold.
161	Today I am going to work.

163	Conjunctions
164	Og (and), men (but), eller (or) and så (so) join two main clauses. Fordi (because), at (that), når (when), hvis (if), selv om (although), mens (while) and før (before) start a subordinate clause — there ikke comes BEFORE the verb: “…fordi jeg ikke har tid”.

166	but
167	or
168	because
169	that
170	if
171	although
172	I am staying home because I am ill.
173	He says that he does not have time.
174	Sin/si/sitt/sine and reflexive verbs
175	Sin/si/sitt/sine means “one’s own” when the owner is the third-person subject: “Kari ringer broren sin” (her own brother), but “Kari ringer broren hennes” (another woman’s brother). Reflexive verbs take seg/meg/deg/oss: “Jeg gleder meg”, “Vi setter oss”.
176	Kari calls her (own) brother.
177	Kari calls her (someone else’s) brother.

183	Present perfect
184	The present perfect is har + past participle. Use it when the result matters now or the action is still going on: “Jeg har bodd i Norge i to år”. With a specific past time (i går, i 2020) use the past tense. Forms: snakket, spist, bodd, gått, vært, hatt, sett, kommet, gjort, sagt.

195	The passive
196	The passive shows that something is done to the subject. Bli + participle for a specific event: “Huset ble bygd i 1990”. The s-passive for rules and regular actions: “Butikken åpnes klokka ni”, “Det snakkes norsk her”. After modals: “Søknaden må sendes i dag”.
